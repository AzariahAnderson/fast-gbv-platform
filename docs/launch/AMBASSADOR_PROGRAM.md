# FAST Community Ambassadors

A low-cost, high-trust programme: real people carrying Fast into the places
they already stand — classrooms, clinics, sports fields, family group chats.

**The one-sentence definition:** a Fast Community Ambassador is a volunteer
who *introduces* people to Fast and *never* replaces the professional
services Fast points to.

---

## Programme explanation

- Ambassadors share the WhatsApp messages, put up approved posters, run a
  10-minute awareness slot at a school assembly or community meeting, and
  hand out cards.
- They are the human face of Fast in their community and the first line of
  quality control: if a poster is defaced, a number looks wrong, or the
  site misbehaves, they report it.
- The programme costs almost nothing: posters, cards, and the time of
  people who already care. No salaries, no vehicles, no offices.
- Ambassadors are **not** employed by Fast; they are independent volunteers
  operating under this code of conduct.

## Eligibility

- 18 years or older.
- Known to the community they want to serve (a school, a ward, a
  congregation, a workplace) — ambassadors start where they already have
  trust, never as strangers with a mission.
- Willing to complete the onboarding below and to be removed from the
  programme if the code of conduct is broken. The conduct rules are not
  guidelines; they are the membership.

## Responsibilities

1. Share Fast with people who can consent and benefit — never cold-call
   strangers in crisis.
2. Use only approved materials from this folder; never design a new poster,
   edit a number, or invent a statistic.
3. Report broken links, wrong information or site problems within 24 hours.
4. Protect privacy: never record who they spoke to, never keep a "case
   list", never photograph a person they helped.
5. Attend a 30-minute refresher once a quarter.

## Onboarding steps

1. Read this document and the [NGO_OUTREACH.md](NGO_OUTREACH.md) one-pager end to end.
2. Use Fast on their own phone for a week: complete the welcome flow, open
   the emergency directory, save the offline page, tap every channel.
3. A 45-minute call with the programme coordinator (role-play: "someone
   asks you for urgent help").
4. Receive the starter kit: 5 posters, 50 cards, the WhatsApp message set.
5. Start in one place only — their own community — and expand later.

## Code of conduct

- **Do no harm.** Never put a person at risk by intervening in a dangerous
  situation, confronting a perpetrator, or telling a survivor to "just leave".
- **Confidentiality without exceptions.** What a person shares stays with
  the ambassador, unless the person is in immediate danger — and even then
  the response is to connect them to the emergency services, not to inform
  a family or community.
- **No money, ever.** Ambassadors do not accept or request payment for
  introductions, and never handle money on another person's behalf.
- **No political or religious framing.** Fast is an independent,
  public-interest resource; ambassadors present it the same way everywhere.
- **No social-media cases.** Never post about a person they helped, even
  anonymously. Success stories are shared only as the person's own choice
  and voice, never the ambassador's narrative.

## Messaging guidelines

Say what Fast **is**: a free website that gathers verified GBV support
services, prevention information and safety-planning guidance in one calm,
private, offline-capable place.

Say what Fast **is not**: not a government service, not a helpline itself,
not a counselling service, not a rescue service, not affiliated with any
political party or religious body.

Keep numbers sacred: always show the number exactly as the site displays
it, or point to the site. An ambassador who is unsure of a number says
"it's on the site" — never "I think it's…".

## Explaining Fast in 30 seconds

> "Fast is a free website — it works on any phone, even offline. It has
> every verified GBV support service in the country: the Command Centre,
> shelters, police, legal help. It also explains warning signs, consent,
> boundaries and how to make a safety plan. No sign-up, nothing stored on
> any server. Have a look, save it, and send it to one person who needs it."

## When someone asks for urgent help

The protocol is always the same, in this order:

1. Stay calm. Speak slowly. Do not touch their phone or take charge of
   their situation.
2. Open Fast together and tap **Get help now** — the verified GBV Command
   Centre number, free, 24/7.
3. If the person cannot speak safely, show them the silent channels
   (please-call-me and SMS) exactly as displayed on the site.
4. If there is immediate physical danger, help them call 10111 (police) or
   112 (any cellphone), as displayed in the Fast directory.
5. Then stop. Hand control back to the person. Do not offer to go with
   them unless they ask, and never follow without their request.

## What ambassadors must NEVER promise

- "They will help you" — promise availability, not outcomes.
- "You will get a protection order" — describe what the site explains, not
  a result.
- "Your information is safe with me" — say instead: "Fast stores nothing;
  and I will keep what you tell me private."
- "I can get you out tonight" — safety planning is the survivor's own
  decisions, informed by the site's guidance and professional services.
- Any timeline, any guarantee, any legal advice, any medical advice.

## Avoiding dangerous advice

If a question needs a professional — legal, medical, psychological, police
matters, immigration, child protection — the ambassador's answer is one
sentence: **"That is exactly what the services on Fast are for — let's open
it together."** The ambassador never diagnoses, never interprets the law,
never assesses someone's mental state, never tells a person whether to
leave, stay, report or forgive. Ambassadors open doors; they do not walk
through them for people.
